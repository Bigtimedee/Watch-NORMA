# Editorial Partnership Pitch — Watch NORMA

**Contact:** press@norma-app.com

## The Pitch Angle

**"The sports app that tells you when to tune in, not just the final score."**

Every major sports app sends you alerts when the game ends. NORMA sends you one alert, mid-game, when something is happening that you specifically need to see — because your bet is live, your team just erased a 14-point deficit, or a star player is in foul trouble with 6 minutes left. Then it routes you directly to the right streaming app with a single tap.

---

## Data Angle (Hypothesis)

NORMA's hypothesis, to be validated as the user base grows, is that sports alert fatigue is near a breaking point. The average fan has ESPN, a sportsbook app, a fantasy app, and a streaming aggregator installed, each sending its own notification stream. We believe there is a measurable gap between "volume of alerts received" and "moments that caused someone to actually change the channel." NORMA is designed to close that gap. As we accumulate alert feedback data (thumbs-up / thumbs-down ratings tied to every alert), we intend to share aggregate findings on which moment types drive the most meaningful viewer engagement with interested editorial partners. This data does not exist yet in publishable form; we are framing it as a forward hypothesis, not a claim.

---

## Story Angles

### 1. The End of the Score Alert

Sports apps have trained fans to ignore notifications. The ESPN badge on your lock screen is ambient noise. NORMA is built on a thesis that the only alert worth sending is one you would have wanted to receive at exactly that moment. The engineering question — how do you score whether a moment is "worth a push" for this specific user? — is the whole product. Angle: what does it mean to rebuild sports media attention from first principles?

### 2. The Bettor's Second Screen

Sports betting is legal in most US states and mainstream in behavior. Tens of millions of Americans have active wagers on any given night of the season. None of the apps they use to place bets tell them when their bet is live in the most consequential sense: the spread is being crossed with 90 seconds left. NORMA is the first app designed specifically for that moment. Angle: what does the infrastructure layer for the betting-native sports fan look like?

### 3. Attention as Inventory

NORMA's ad engine does not sell banner impressions. It sells "the moment a user with an active DraftKings bet sees their spread going the wrong way." That is a categorically different advertising product. The Vickrey auction clears in under 50 milliseconds. The floor price for that moment type is set algorithmically based on historical fill rates. Angle: how a 10-person sports app built a programmatic ad marketplace that DSPs and media buyers can access via AI agent protocol (MCP).

## General Pitch Notes

- Always lead with the user problem, not the technology: the problem is notification fatigue plus the fragmentation between sportsbook, streaming app, and score app.
- The strongest press proof point is a live alert walkthrough — offer a demo call where the reporter can see a real alert fire during a live game.
- For outlets that care about the business model: NORMA's advertising infrastructure (Vickrey auction, MCP server, Programmatic Intent API) is a genuinely novel B2B story, distinct from the consumer app story.
- For outlets that care about the technology: the "Why Now" explanation engine — scoring each alert against a weighted signal vector and generating a structured headline and bullets — is worth explaining.
- Do not claim user numbers, engagement rates, or revenue figures you cannot verify. Lead with the product and the architecture.
